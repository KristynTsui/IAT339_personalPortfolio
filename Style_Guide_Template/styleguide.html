Biography

Hello! My name is Kristyn, and I am a designer that has a strong interest in storytelling and using colour themes with shapes to add more dimension. I love collaborative projects that allow me to join my skills with others and use up a lot of space when designing. I like my colours to be calm in saturation and to keep my shapes more round with text that includes mostly elegant serifs. I prefer bigger and bolder text for easy readability, and I enjoy experimenting with lots of images and different styles of speaking while making publications.

Project #1: Turnish

Pot Bound: The Turnish Relay began with an idea I pitched to my team: two vegetables racing to avoid becoming soup. My ethos is about warmth and approachability supported by a cohesive visual system, and this premise let me test both. Turnip and Radish needed to feel friendly at a glance, so we gave them rounded forms, stubby legs, and minimal facial features. Having them waddle instead of run was deliberate. It adds humour and keeps their rivalry playful.

I started with the storyboard, drawing it live during a team call while we worked through the plot. That allowed the team to collaborate even during an individual task and let us settle the story beats before any modelling began. [storyboard image here]

Next, I modelled part of the kitchen while teammates built the rest and laid down base colours. I then compiled every model, finished the colouring and texturing, and began animating. [greyed model kitchen image]

The main design problem was keeping a busy countertop from overwhelming two small characters. My solution was reuse: a limited set of colours and textures repeated across surfaces, so the kitchen stayed cohesive and the characters stayed the focus. With such minimal faces, emotion had to come from body language, so I animated glares, posture, and timing to carry the rivalry across the race.

I worked in Maya with V-Ray and Photoshop, rendered the final animation, and passed it to a teammate for effects and sound design. This project shows my ability to take an idea from concept to final render, collaborate across stages, and build a consistent visual system that serves the story.

Project #2: Sonder Over Yonder

Sonder Over Yonder is a transformative board game aimed at making players feel that everyone around them carries a story as layered as their own. That belief about empathy and connection sits at the center of my ethos, so the design had to feel personal and inviting while staying organized enough to play.

We began by brainstorming theme ideas, where I initially gave the idea of the 7 degrees of separation. This gradually moved to interconnective mapping, leading to the thoughts about loneliness and how self-preserving our current world is.

The game offers players 16 unique characters, each with 3 unlockable stories, 5 possible endings, and endless trait combinations to play through. That scale meant I needed a system to keep it readable. I used Figma to set up all the game piece designs, formatting huge maps of cards while also helping with the story writing and character creation. I operated as a project manager and also the graphic designer, creating all the game piece layouts and then printing them out for cutting. We then moved to the more difficult elements, producing the game board and character stands in laser-cut acrylic and wood.

Our team conducted 3 playtests over the course of our project. These taught me a lot about how each player would navigate the game differently, and no two plays were alike. I also suggested revisions to our core reward system for if players chose to help each other or get ahead alone, since the final piece asks players to decide whether to wander alone or wander together, so the design had to reward both choices.

This project shows my ability to turn an emotional idea into a structured, tactile experience, to test and refine it through iteration, and to move between digital tools and physical production. It also shows what my work aims for: designs that feel warm and personal but are built on a clear system.
